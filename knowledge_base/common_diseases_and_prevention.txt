# WHO and MedlinePlus Guide: Common Chronic and Infectious Diseases Prevention
Source: World Health Organization (WHO) & MedlinePlus Public Health Education
Topic: Chronic Diseases and Infection Prevention

## 1. Type 2 Diabetes Overview and Prevention
Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic condition where the body becomes resistant to insulin or does not produce enough insulin, leading to elevated blood glucose levels.
Common Symptoms:
- Increased thirst (polydipsia) and frequent urination, especially at night
- Unexplained weight loss and increased hunger
- Chronic fatigue and low energy
- Blurred vision and slow-healing sores or cuts
- Frequent infections, such as gum or skin infections
Prevention Strategies:
- Healthy Diet: Emphasize whole grains, fiber-rich vegetables, legumes, and lean proteins while limiting refined sugars, sweetened beverages, and saturated fats.
- Regular Physical Activity: Strive for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise per week (such as brisk walking or cycling) combined with muscle-strengthening activities.
- Weight Management: Maintaining a healthy body weight or losing 5% to 7% of excess body weight significantly decreases diabetes progression risk.
- Routine Screening: Adults aged 35 and older, or those with risk factors (family history, gestational diabetes, obesity), should have regular fasting blood glucose or HbA1c screenings.

## 2. Hypertension (High Blood Pressure) Awareness
Hypertension is often referred to as a "silent killer" because it frequently produces no obvious symptoms until organ damage occurs.
Normal Blood Pressure: Typically under 120/80 mmHg. Persistent readings above 130/80 mmHg generally warrant medical evaluation.
Risk Factors:
- High dietary sodium intake (more than 2 grams of sodium or 5 grams of salt per day)
- Lack of physical exercise, obesity, and chronic psychological stress
- Tobacco smoking and excessive alcohol consumption
Lifestyle Interventions:
- Dietary Approaches to Stop Hypertension (DASH diet): Rich in fruits, vegetables, low-fat dairy, potassium, and magnesium.
- Reduce sodium intake to less than 1,500 - 2,000 mg per day.
- Regular blood pressure self-monitoring with a validated home cuff.

## 3. Respiratory Infections: Influenza vs. Common Cold
Both influenza (flu) and the common cold are viral respiratory infections, but they differ in severity:
- Common Cold: Typically develops gradually. Symptoms include runny or stuffy nose, mild sore throat, sneezing, and low or absent fever.
- Influenza (Flu): Symptoms usually appear abruptly and severely. Characterized by high fever (38°C/100.4°F or higher), intense muscle or body aches, severe fatigue, headache, chills, and a dry cough.
Prevention & Hygiene:
- Annual Influenza Vaccination: Recommended for individuals aged 6 months and older, especially high-risk populations (older adults, pregnant women, immunocompromised).
- Proper Hand Hygiene: Washing hands thoroughly with soap and clean water for at least 20 seconds.
- Respiratory Etiquette: Covering mouth and nose with a tissue or flexed elbow when coughing or sneezing.
- Staying Home: Isolating when sick to prevent transmission to vulnerable individuals.
