# NHS and MedlinePlus Guide: General Symptoms, Causes, and When to Seek Care
Source: National Health Service (NHS) & MedlinePlus Patient Information
Topic: Symptom Awareness and Triage

## 1. Fever in Adults
A normal body temperature is approximately 37°C (98.6°F). A fever is generally defined as a body temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or above.
Possible Causes:
- Viral infections (flu, rhinovirus, COVID-19, gastrointestinal bugs)
- Bacterial infections (urinary tract infections, strep throat, pneumonia)
- Inflammatory conditions or vaccine immune responses
Self-Care Advice:
- Stay well hydrated by drinking clear fluids, oral rehydration solutions, or broths.
- Rest adequately and wear lightweight clothing.
- Over-the-counter antipyretics (e.g., paracetamol/acetaminophen or ibuprofen) can alleviate discomfort if medically appropriate.
When to Seek Urgent Medical Care:
- A fever exceeding 39.4°C (103°F) in adults, or lasting more than 3 consecutive days.
- Fever accompanied by a stiff neck, confusion, unexplained rash, or severe difficulty breathing.

## 2. Headaches: Tension, Migraine, and Red Flags
Headaches are common and vary in etiology and severity.
- Tension Headaches: The most common type; presents as a dull, steady ache or pressure band around the forehead or back of the head. Often triggered by stress, dehydration, poor posture, or lack of sleep.
- Migraines: Moderate to severe throbbing pain, often unilateral, frequently accompanied by sensitivity to light (photophobia), sound, and nausea.
Emergency Warning Signs ("Thunderclap" or Red Flag Headaches):
- A sudden, excruciating headache reaching maximum intensity within seconds or minutes.
- Headache following a recent head injury.
- Headache accompanied by neurological signs: facial drooping, arm weakness, slurred speech, confusion, or visual loss. These require immediate emergency hospital triage (call 911 or 112).

## 3. Persistent Fatigue and Low Energy
Fatigue is an extreme, persistent feeling of tiredness or exhaustion not relieved by sleep.
Common Non-Urgent Causes:
- Inadequate or irregular sleep patterns (sleep deprivation, sleep apnea)
- Poor nutritional intake, iron deficiency anemia, or vitamin D deficiency
- Chronic emotional stress, anxiety, or depression
- Sedentary lifestyle or dehydration
Evaluation:
- If unexplained fatigue persists for more than 2 to 4 weeks despite adequate sleep, a doctor should be consulted for comprehensive blood tests (thyroid panel, CBC, ferritin, metabolic screen).

## 4. Cough: Acute vs. Persistent
- Acute Cough (lasting less than 3 weeks): Frequently caused by viral upper respiratory tract infections, post-nasal drip, or acute bronchitis.
- Chronic Cough (lasting 8 weeks or longer in adults): Common causes include asthma, gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD), chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD), environmental allergies, or medication side effects (such as ACE inhibitors).
Urgent Warning Signs:
- Coughing up blood (hemoptysis).
- Shortness of breath, audible wheezing, or persistent chest pain.

## 5. Hand, Wrist, and Arm Pain: Causes, Ergonomics, and Triage
Hand and arm discomfort can arise from muscles, tendons, ligaments, joints, or peripheral nerves.
Common Non-Emergency Causes:
- Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI) and Overuse: Prolonged computer typing, mouse use, manual assembly work, or repetitive gripping leading to wrist or hand tendon irritation, stiffness, and mild ache.
- Sprains and Strains: Overstretching wrist or finger ligaments or hand muscles during physical work, exercise, or minor awkward movements.
- Carpal Tunnel Syndrome: Compression of the median nerve within the carpal tunnel of the wrist. Symptoms include tingling, numbness, burning, or aching pain in the thumb, index, middle fingers, and palm, frequently worsening at night or after repetitive hand movements.
- Tendonitis / Tenosynovitis (e.g., De Quervain's): Pain and tenderness along the wrist and base of the thumb aggravated by pinching or gripping.
- Arthritis: Joint stiffness, swelling, or dull aching in the knuckles, fingers, or wrist, characteristically more pronounced upon waking in the morning.
Self-Care and First-Line Management:
- Ergonomics and Rest: Take scheduled micro-breaks from typing or repetitive tasks every 30 to 60 minutes. Use ergonomic keyboard and wrist rests, and avoid forceful sustained gripping.
- Temperature Therapy: Apply a cool pack wrapped in a cloth for 15 to 20 minutes to reduce acute swelling or inflammation; use gentle warmth for chronic muscle stiffness.
- Gentle Range-of-Motion Exercises: Perform gentle wrist rotations and finger extensions without forcing movement past the threshold of pain.
- Temporary Splinting: A neutral wrist splint worn during sleep or repetitive activities can alleviate nerve compression in early carpal tunnel symptoms.
When to Seek Medical Evaluation:
- Pain or numbness that persists for more than a few days, worsens over time, or interferes with sleep and daily activities.
- Inability to grip objects, significant loss of hand strength, or visible joint swelling, redness, and warmth.
- Critical Red Flag: Any left arm or hand pain that is accompanied by chest tightness, pressure, or pain, shortness of breath, cold sweating, nausea, lightheadedness, or sudden onset requires immediate emergency evaluation (call 112 or local emergency services).
