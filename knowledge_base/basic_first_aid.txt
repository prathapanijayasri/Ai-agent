# NHS and Red Cross First Aid Reference: Basic First Aid and Emergency Action
Source: National Health Service (NHS) & International Federation of Red Cross First Aid Guidelines
Topic: Basic First Aid Protocols

## 1. Minor Burns and Scalds First Aid
Minor burns (superficial/first-degree) involve only the epidermis and cause redness, localized swelling, and pain.
Immediate Actions:
- Cool Immediately: Hold the affected area under cool running water (not ice or freezing water) for at least 10 to 20 minutes. Cooling stops the burning process and relieves pain.
- Remove Jewelry: Gently remove tight items such as rings, watches, or clothing before swelling starts (do not remove clothing stuck to burned skin).
- Do Not Apply Ice, Butter, Toothpaste, or Oils: These trap heat, damage delicate tissues, and introduce infection risks.
- Cover Loosely: Use clean, non-stick sterile gauze or clean plastic cling film placed loosely over the area.
- Do Not Pop Blisters: Intact blister skin serves as a sterile barrier against bacterial infection.
Seek Professional Medical Attention If:
- The burn is larger than the palm of the casualty's hand.
- The burn covers the face, hands, feet, groin, or major joint.
- The burn is chemical or electrical, or shows white, charred, or blackened skin (third-degree).

## 2. Sprains and Strains: The R.I.C.E. Protocol
Sprains involve injured ligaments, while strains involve stretched or torn muscles and tendons.
The R.I.C.E. Protocol:
- Rest: Protect the injured limb from further movement or weight-bearing.
- Ice: Apply a cold pack wrapped in a damp towel for 15 to 20 minutes every 2 to 3 hours during the initial 48 hours. Never place ice directly on bare skin.
- Compression: Wrap the joint with an elastic bandage firmly, but not so tight that it obstructs arterial circulation or causes tingling/numbness.
- Elevation: Elevate the injured area above heart level whenever resting to minimize interstitial fluid accumulation and swelling.
Seek Medical Evaluation If:
- Unable to bear any weight on the limb.
- Severe deformity, joint instability, or severe numbness below the injury.

## 3. Cuts, Scrapes, and Minor Bleeding
Steps for Minor Wounds:
- Wash Hands: Always wash hands with soap and water or wear disposable gloves before touching wounds.
- Apply Direct Pressure: Use a clean cloth or sterile gauze pad and maintain continuous pressure for several minutes to arrest bleeding.
- Clean the Wound: Rinse the wound gently with clean tap water. Mild soap can be used around the wound margins. Avoid harsh chemicals like hydrogen peroxide or iodine directly inside open tissue as they delay cellular healing.
- Protect: Apply a thin layer of petroleum jelly or antibiotic ointment and cover with a sterile adhesive dressing. Change dressing daily or if it becomes wet/dirty.
- Tetanus Booster: Consult a healthcare professional if the last tetanus vaccination was more than 5 to 10 years ago or if the injury was caused by dirty or rusty objects.

## 4. Epistaxis (Nosebleeds) Management
Correct Position:
- Sit Upright and Lean Forward Slightly: Leaning back allows blood to drain into the throat, which can cause vomiting or airway irritation.
- Pinch Soft Area: Pinch the soft part of the nose (just below the bony bridge) continuously with thumb and index finger for 10 to 15 minutes without releasing.
- Breathe Through Mouth: Remain calm and breathe quietly through the mouth.
- Cold Compress: An ice pack placed over the bridge of the nose or forehead may help constrict blood vessels.
Seek Emergency Care If:
- Bleeding continues uninterrupted for more than 20 minutes of firm pressure.
- Bleeding is extremely heavy, or resulted from major facial trauma, or causes dizziness and fainting.
